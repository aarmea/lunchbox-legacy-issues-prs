## Description

Follow-up on [#121](./0121.md)

The connect-drain bound regressed reconnect-after-restart: the app
would never re-establish a session once the initial pair's connection
was gone, load or no load.

Reconnecting to a bonded peer gives you a GATT link that isn't
encrypted yet. Android sets encryption up lazily from the stored LTK,
triggered by the first ATT request that needs it — which is the drain's
first read. That read can take seconds, and can fail outright and
succeed on retry, with a perfectly healthy bond. Timing it against
DRAIN_BUDGET_MS meant a slow handshake read as a stalled drain
(disconnect, retry, repeat) and a failed one read as a dead bond.

This only surfaced after the initial pair because pairing bonds the
link in place and adopt() reuses it; the first app restart is the first
fresh connection to an already-bonded peer.

- readAfterSettle: the session's first read per characteristic gets its
  own 5s budget and 3 attempts. DRAIN_BUDGET_MS (now 5s) starts after
  it lands, so it only bounds steady-state draining.
- CONNECT_TIMEOUT_MS 20s -> 30s, above the sum of connect()'s internal
  budgets, so stalls surface as the specific failure rather than a
  generic timeout.
- Offer Re-pair on the Disconnected banner too. Nothing auto-unbonds
  any more, so when the scan probe can't run that banner was a dead end
  for a one-sided bond.
